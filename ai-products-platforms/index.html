FOUR STARTING POINTS

Possibility, with boundaries.

Expand each concept for its intended workflow, limitations, and evaluation questions.

01 / FIND

Knowledge Compass

A concept for finding answers inside a carefully governed collection of working documents.

Explore Knowledge Compass

The intended workflow

An employee asks a question about a process. The system retrieves only documents they are permitted to access, drafts an answer, and links to the exact source passages for review.

Where it could help

Internal policy navigation and technical handovers where source material is maintained and an accountable owner can resolve ambiguity.

What must be tested

  • Whether retrieval respects document-level permissions, including after access changes.
  • Whether cited passages actually support each answer.
  • Whether missing or conflicting evidence produces an explicit uncertainty message rather than a guess.

This is not a source of legal advice or a substitute for the document owner.

02 / INTERPRET

Signal Studio

A concept for turning operational data into questions worth investigating, not automatic conclusions.

Explore Signal Studio

The intended workflow

An analyst selects an approved dataset and time window. The tool highlights unusual patterns, shows the underlying records, and proposes possible explanations to investigate.

Where it could help

Exploring service demand or inventory variation before deciding whether an operational change is justified.

What must be tested

  • Missing data, seasonal patterns, and changes to collection methods.
  • False alerts and overlooked events against a simple statistical baseline.
  • Clear separation of observed correlation from an unverified causal explanation.

A human analyst owns the interpretation. No changes to business systems would happen automatically.

03 / PREPARE

Workflow Canvas

A concept for drafting routine work while keeping review and execution separate.

Explore Workflow Canvas

The intended workflow

A service coordinator supplies an approved brief. The tool suggests a checklist and a response draft, highlighting assumptions and any missing information. The coordinator edits and approves the work.

Where it could help

Preparing handover notes or first-pass service responses in low-risk, repeatable workflows.

What must be tested

  • Whether important details survive summarisation without invented additions.
  • Whether untrusted input can redirect the task or expose restricted information.
  • Whether reviewers can inspect, change, or reject every proposed action.

The concept does not send messages, approve requests, or execute actions on a person's behalf.

04 / EXAMINE

Evaluation Bench

A concept for comparing an AI-assisted workflow with the process it is meant to improve.

Explore Evaluation Bench

The intended workflow

A product team assembles representative and adversarial test cases, agrees scoring criteria with domain reviewers, and compares candidate configurations against a non-AI baseline.

Where it could help

Deciding whether an assistant is ready for a limited pilot, or whether a model, prompt, or source update introduces regressions.

What must be tested

  • Task quality, privacy failures, latency, cost, and accessibility together.
  • Performance across languages and user groups relevant to the intended setting.
  • Versioned datasets, independent review, and reproducible failure cases.

A passing score would support a scoped decision, not certify a system as universally safe.

EVIDENCE BEFORE EXPANSION

Earn the next step.

A proposed evaluation approach, not a claim of certification or production readiness.

  1. 01

    Define the boundary

    Agree the task, affected people, data permissions, retention limits, and prohibited uses. Decide whether AI is necessary at all. Name an accountable owner.

  2. 02

    Test the difficult cases

    Use consented or synthetic examples. Measure unsupported answers, privacy leakage, bias, and failure recovery alongside task quality. Set acceptance thresholds before testing.

  3. 03

    Keep control visible

    For any future pilot, require human review, clear disclosure, monitoring, an escalation route, and a tested stop mechanism. Re-evaluate after meaningful changes.
